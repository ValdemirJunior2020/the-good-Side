The Atacama Desert blooms in 2025

Just a few weeks ago it was the driest place on Earth. Now it’s the most colorful.

Chile’s Atacama Desert has erupted in an extraordinary floral display, with around 200 species blooming after rare rains awakened seeds that had lain dormant for decades.

The transformation is a remarkable reminder of the resilience of life. Scientists and conservationists are collecting seeds, studying the desert’s biodiversity, and working to protect the ecosystem.

“It’s as if the desert is awakening.”— Ana Sandoval, as quoted in reporting about the superbloom
ENVIRONMENT6 min read · Featured story

When the driest desert comes alive

The rare rains reached seeds and tubers deep beneath the sands of Chile’s Atacama. With roughly 200 flowering species observed, parts of the desert displayed color that residents and scientists had not seen for decades.

Protected areas such as Llanos de Challe National Park conserve fragile habitats during this short biological window. Researchers at Chile’s Initihuasi Seed Bank are gathering seeds from newly pollinated plants to study and safeguard the region’s biodiversity for the future.

The phenomenon is both a celebration and a reminder: even an ecosystem that appears dormant can hold extraordinary life waiting for the right conditions.
